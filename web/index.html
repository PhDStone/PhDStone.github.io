Research 2024–26

RuNi–M (M = Mo/Cr/W) Alloys — Activity through Dynamic Reconstruction

Details

Background

High-activity Ru-based alkaline HER catalysts do not hold still — their surfaces restructure under operating conditions, and that behavior is where the activity comes from.

Role

Alloy synthesis, electrochemical characterization, and data analysis across the RuNi–M composition series, with EchemPro as the analysis backbone.

Outcome

Identified composition–activity patterns in the high-activity RuNiMo system and traced them to dynamic surface reconstruction — the finding that seeded my thesis' unifying theme: dynamic behavior under real operating conditions.

Research 2025–26

Ru/NiMo Heterojunction — Stability against Reverse Current

Details

Background

In real alkaline electrolysis setups, catalysts must survive reverse-current events — not just steady-state operation. Activity without stability does not ship.

Role

Heterostructure design, accelerated stress testing, and decay diagnostics to separate intrinsic degradation from event-driven damage.

Outcome

Developed a Ru/NiMo heterojunction catalyst engineered for anti-reverse-current stability — the stability counterpart to the activity work, completing the activity–stability pair at the core of my thesis.
